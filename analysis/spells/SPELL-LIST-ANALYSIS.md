# Spell list and eligibility analysis

The 969 spell definition roots do **not** contain class or subclass spell-list membership fields. There is no spell-list root collection or auxiliary spell-list file in the recursive spell directory. `referenceSources`, `otherSources`, `additionalSources`, and `reprintedAs` are source relationships, not class eligibility.

Eligibility-like declarations are elsewhere in the corpus. Classes and features use typed `additionalSpells` grants and selectors; the completed class discovery records **186 selector occurrences across 102 distinct expressions**, including level, class, school, and source constraints. Selectors describe a candidate set and do not enumerate membership or necessarily identify an actual grant. Other catalogs preserve spell schedules or grants: Feats have `additionalSpells` on 86 roots; Backgrounds preserve 15 grant groups; Optional Features preserve 54; Races preserve structured `additionalSpells`; Items preserve attached-spell schedules. These are grants or choices, not a canonical complete class spell list. Monsters' spellcasting data is a creature-specific prepared/known/usage representation.

Do not derive eligibility from spell prose or a name. Preserve grant owner identity, selector expression, source qualifiers, level gates, categories, and choice counts from the owning catalog. A future eligibility endpoint may need to aggregate these authored schedules with a deliberately sourced class-list dataset; Stage A found no standalone complete list source in `data/spells/`.

An exact source-qualified spell reference can be checked against `(name, source)` without fuzzy matching. An unqualified name may collide across sources: 386 names repeat across the corpus, including 359 PHB/XPHB pairs. Therefore unqualified grants cannot be assigned to a unique spell definition in general. `all Wizard cantrips`-style expressions are filters, not collections of individual references and should stay distinct from spell IDs.
