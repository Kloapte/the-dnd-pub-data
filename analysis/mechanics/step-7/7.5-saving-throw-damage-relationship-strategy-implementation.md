# Step 7.5 — Saving Throw to Damage Relationship Strategy

## Result

Implemented the first reusable relationship family for the experimental mechanics pipeline:
an authored saving throw outcome can govern an extracted damage mechanic when the source text
establishes that relationship. The strategy has no spell-owner or source-edition allowlist.
Canonical extraction, canonical claim identities, human review, licensing, and publication
authorization remain unchanged. Step 7.5 remains in progress.

## Design and implementation

The pipeline now runs a distinct relationship-composition stage after mechanic extraction.
`MechanicRelationshipStrategy` defines the bounded strategy contract, and
`MechanicRelationshipStrategyRegistry` registers and orders strategies deterministically.
`MechanicInterpretationContext` provides the source-qualified owner, pinned revision, source
fragments, and evidence index. `SavingThrowDamageOutcomeStrategy` is the first implementation.

The strategy links only an explicit outcome clause to an extracted save and damage mechanic.
It handles failure/full damage, success/half damage, success/no damage, and separate authored
damage amounts for save outcomes. A successful-save clause is left unresolved if its damage
application cannot be determined. Save/damage co-occurrence alone produces a diagnostic, not an
edge. If multiple saves or damage components make a pairing unclear, the strategy abstains and
reports the ambiguity. One save can relate independently to multiple damage components.

Save extraction now uses authored prose to establish the saving-throw meaning and does not
mistake an ability check for a saving throw. Difficulty remains explicitly unknown when the
source does not state a numeric DC. Damage extraction supports multiple tagged damage formulas
with an adjacent authored type; structured damage-type tags alone do not create a damage event.
Damage resistance and vulnerability are not converted into direct damage. Unsupported scaling
remains diagnostic.

Stable relationship IDs include the source-qualified owner, source resource fingerprint,
strategy identity, and endpoint mechanic IDs and outcomes. Each relationship carries exact local
mechanic references, a source revision, outcome/application rules, and evidence references.
`ExperimentalMechanicsV2Dto` validates unique IDs, same-owner and same-source references,
endpoint families, evidence reciprocity, evidence support for both endpoints, and source revision
consistency. Relationships are top-level in the experimental v2 projection; mechanics remain
mechanical payloads with context, and evidence remains separately addressable. Canonical
interpretations remain in their existing projection.

The extraction strategy is intentionally bounded. It recognizes supported outcome wording and
tagged damage expressions; it is not a general natural-language parser or runtime save resolver.
Source clauses outside those forms remain explicit diagnostics. A relationship is emitted per
unambiguously governed damage mechanic, not per creature or runtime resolution.

## Focused examples

- `spell:phb:fireball` produces one `SAVING_THROW_DAMAGE_OUTCOME` relationship: save failure
  applies full damage; save success applies half damage. Its three canonical claims remain
  independent and unchanged.
- `spell:au:detonate` produces two independent relationships from its separate source-backed
  save/damage clauses.
- `spell:phb:prismatic-spray` produces no relationship for the ambiguous clause because the
  governing save is not unique; it retains `AMBIGUOUS_SAVE_DAMAGE_PAIRING`.
- `spell:au:vision-of-elapsing-eons` has save and damage in the same source text but no explicit
  outcome clause; it produces no relationship and retains `SAVE_DAMAGE_CO_OCCURRENCE_ONLY`.

## Remaining relationship work

The next source-backed relationship families should be migrated independently and in this order:

1. Attack outcome to damage application (including HIT/MISS-gated effects).
2. Attack-triggered effects that occur regardless of attack outcome.
3. Save outcome to condition or other non-damage effects.
4. Spell-slot or character-level scaling to the exact affected mechanic.
5. Persistent-effect establishment, activation, and termination relationships.

Each requires a separate source-backed rule, ambiguity handling, validation, and tests before it
is enabled. This step does not implement those families or promote experimental output to
canonical mechanics.
