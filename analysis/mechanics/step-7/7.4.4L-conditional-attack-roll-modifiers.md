## Preimplementation model decision

The existing model has no advantage/disadvantage value. `StatisticModifierMechanic` expresses numeric changes and cannot stand for roll quality. `AttackRollMechanic` describes a roll but cannot carry an independent conditional modifier without mixing attack identity, effect, condition and activation. `AppliesOnAttackRollOutcome` expresses an outcome dependency, so it fits Chill Touch's HIT trigger, but it would be wrong for Shocking Grasp's pre-hit advantage. `AppliesWhen` is reusable for source conditions, but its current condition enum lacks both the undead-subject and worn-metal-armor cases. The existing `MechanicRevisionReference`, `AppliesForDuration`, `UntilTurnBoundary`, and `TurnActor.CASTER`/`AFFECTED_SUBJECT` fit the required relationship and duration concepts.

`CreatureTypeRestriction` currently has only `HUMANOID`; using a target restriction would falsely imply that Chill Touch itself only targets undead. No equipment or armor qualification model exists. The condition must therefore remain a separate applicability binding on the modifier claim.

The selected smallest reusable shape is a new `AttackRollModifierMechanic` payload containing an `ADVANTAGE` or `DISADVANTAGE` value plus a typed `AttackRollSelector`. A specific-roll selector holds the exact referenced attack claim revision for Shocking Grasp. A future-roll selector names `AFFECTED_SUBJECT` as actor and `CASTER` as target for Chill Touch; these future attack rolls have no existing claim IDs to reference. `AppliesWhen` conditions attach to the modifier record's subject and specify either that subject is undead or that the selected attack's target is wearing armor made of metal. Chill Touch additionally uses its one-way `AppliesOnAttackRollOutcome(HIT)` dependency and an existing caster-owned end-of-next-turn duration. Shocking Grasp has no outcome binding or duration for the advantage.

These edges all point from the modifier claim to an existing attack claim, condition evidence or duration; the attack claims have no reverse references. A future-roll selector is descriptive and does not fabricate claims or implement combat resolution. If validation cannot preserve these distinct roles, the corresponding K diagnostic will remain.

## Implementation

Added `AttackRollModifierMechanic` with an explicit `ADVANTAGE`/`DISADVANTAGE` value and sealed
`AttackRollSelector` variants. `ReferencedAttackRollSelector` stores a revision-bound reference for
an existing attack claim; `FutureAttackRollSelector` names the actor and target roles of later rolls
without inventing attack IDs. `AppliesWhen` and typed applicability conditions carry the separate
qualifier. `AppliesOnAttackRollOutcome` and `AppliesForDuration` remain independent activation and
duration bindings. The document validator requires referenced attacks to exist at the referenced
revision and rejects the incompatible combination of a metal-wearing target and a not-wearing-armor
qualifier. No reverse dependency or combat-resolution behavior was added.
