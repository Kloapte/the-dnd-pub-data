# Step 7.4.4F — Spell Attack-Roll Modeling and Feasibility

**Status: COMPLETE (analysis only)**  
**Source corpus:** pinned 5eTools v2.37.0 Spell records  
**Scope:** attack-roll and hit-dependent mechanics; no production extraction or model change

## Baseline and method

The current 7.4.4E inventory preview verifies 969 source-aware Spell identities: 168
`PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`, 435 `UNSUPPORTED`, 366 `NOT_ASSESSED`, and 0 `SUPPORTED`. It records 114
processed owners and 317 potential typed claims; the current production document count remains 235
typed claims, as recorded by the E report and roadmap. The zero `SUPPORTED` count remains the known
coverage-accounting limitation, not a result of this analysis.

The 7.4.4D family summary reports 75 attack-roll cue owners (19 partial, 56 unsupported, none
unassessed at D's baseline): 6 structured-only, 10 prose-only, and 59 with both modes. Its JSON
contains only five example owners, not the 75 owner IDs or their pointers. Consequently, the old
19/56 classification split cannot be safely recomputed against the post-E inventory, and the ten
prose-only owners cannot all be individually resolved from that artifact. I treat 75 as the
discovery starting count, not as 75 verified eligible spell attacks.

I independently scanned all 969 pinned Spell records and verified 65 distinct owners with a
non-empty structured `spellAttack` field. This count is reproducible from the `spellAttack` property
across the edition-specific `spells-*.json` files. The field is a cue, not a semantic verdict: for
example, TCE Booming Blade has a structured `M` marker but its prose calls for a melee **weapon**
attack. Other prose-only discovery cues can be attack modifiers, attacks made by a target or created
creature, and trigger descriptions. The source-level evidence mode totals reconcile to D's
65 = 59 mixed + 6 structured-only and 75 = 65 + 10 prose-only; the missing owner-level export is
the limitation on more granular recategorization.

Prose was checked in authored `entries` and `entriesHigherLevel`, with structured properties kept
separate. Counts below are distinct owners within the named evidence group; families overlap. A
source pointer is rooted at the edition file's top-level `spell` array. Spell IDs include source and
name, so same-named PHB/TCE/XGE/XPHB definitions remain separate owners.

References to attack rolls as a restriction, condition, example, or trigger are not automatically
attack-roll payloads. For example, the attack made by an affected target under Crown of Madness is a
forced action; Sanctuary and Bless govern other actors' rolls; an attack-roll mention in a
spell-ending clause describes an ending trigger. Nested creature/object text must not be attributed
to the caster without source evidence.

## Existing canonical model fit

| Existing type | What it already represents | Gap for attack-roll modeling |
|---|---|---|
| `DamageMechanic` | Typed damage amount and damage-type selection | No attack action, hit/miss result, or association to the attack roll that gates the damage. |
| `HitByAttackTrigger` and `AttackEventKind` | An event trigger, currently `ATTACK_HIT` or `ATTACK_ROLL_HIT` | They do not describe the roll that produced the event: attack kind, acting entity, number of rolls, target selection, timing, or roll rules. They are used as triggers (for example, reaction activation); a hit trigger is not an attack-roll claim. |
| `SpellTargetSubject` and `TargetScope` | Selected creature target shapes, area-affected creatures, caster/touched creature, optional creature restriction and maximum target count | No objects, target-per-attack allocation, same/different target rule, or actor identity for a summoned entity. |
| `StatisticModifierMechanic` / `ModifiedStatistic.ATTACK_ROLL` | Add/subtract and other typed numeric operations on attack rolls | No advantage/disadvantage, cover interaction, replacement of a roll, or attack-specific eligibility/timing binding. |
| `AppliesOnSaveOutcome`, `ConditionOnSaveOutcome`, `IncludesTriggeringAttack`, `MechanicRevisionReference` | Save outcome dependencies, a narrow triggering-attack inclusion for damage prevention, and revision references | No general hit/miss/critical dependency from a damage or condition claim to the attack-roll claim that caused it. `AppliesOnSaveOutcome` is not reusable for attacks. |
| `EffectDurationMechanic`, `AppliesForDuration`, `ActivatedBy`, and ending bindings | Duration, concentration, activation, and some ending conditions | No typed timing/repeat schedule for multiple attack activations within the duration. |
| `MechanicRecord`, evidence associations, derivation, assessment, review and licensing | Immutable revision-hashed claims; source evidence is required; reference integrity is validated; review/licensing defaults remain pending/unassessed | These are appropriate safeguards and should be retained. Any new attack payload/binding must participate in claim hashing, evidence validation, stale-reference checks and private inspection serialization. |

`ATTACK_HIT` versus `ATTACK_ROLL_HIT` currently does not close these gaps. Its distinction must be
documented before any shared trigger semantics are broadened. `SpellTargetSubject` and the evidence
and review boundaries should remain intact; the issue is missing attack-specific typed data, not a
need to replace the canonical claim structure.

## Smallest viable extension

Add one canonical `AttackRollMechanic` payload and one bounded outcome binding for other claims.
Keep the attack roll itself as a source-backed proposed claim with a stable owner-and-slot mechanic
ID. The payload needs typed values for:

1. **Attack kind:** melee/ranged and spell/weapon distinction; preserve unknown as unknown rather
   than infer it from `spellAttack` alone.
2. **Acting entity:** caster, spell-created entity, or another source-named/selected actor. Do not
   encode an entity attack as a caster attack.
3. **Count and independence:** fixed count for the bounded first batch, whether each roll is
   independent, and an optional maximum when the source says “up to.” Do not force level-scaling or
   arbitrary repeat schedules into an integer field.
4. **Targeting:** creature/object eligibility and whether each attack selects one target, attacks
   distinct targets, or may reuse a target. Reuse `SpellTargetSubject` only for the creature scope
   it actually describes; do not stretch it to objects.
5. **Timing/repetition:** cast-time versus a later source-specified action during duration. Duration
   bindings continue to point to the declared duration; the attack payload should state only the
   attack activation timing supported by prose.
6. **Explicit roll rules and outcomes:** typed critical threshold only when stated; a separate
   `AttackRollOutcome` (`HIT`, `MISS`, and explicitly sourced `CRITICAL_HIT`) for effect dependencies.

Add `AppliesOnAttackRollOutcome(attackRollRevision, outcome)` as a context binding on a related
damage, condition, or other effect claim. This creates a one-way, revision-bound dependency from the
effect to the attack roll, and avoids duplicating damage/condition payloads inside the new attack
payload or making a bidirectional claim cycle. A no-effect miss path should remain absent; a miss
effect must have its own evidenced claim and `MISS` binding. The model describes source-stated
mechanics; it must not calculate hit probabilities, resolve combat, or infer a critical hit.

Keep complex source wording as unsupported diagnostics until its required value types are modeled.
In particular, attack-count scaling by character level, arbitrary repeated activations, target
objects, attack-roll advantage/disadvantage, roll replacement, and mixed hit/miss consequence trees
are not safe to approximate with generic maps or free-text payload fields.

### Proposed canonical JSON examples — NOT IMPLEMENTED

These examples illustrate the proposed shape only. IDs, hashes, evidence IDs, and revision hashes
are placeholders; they are not production claims or canonical serialized output.

**A. One melee spell attack; damage only on hit — PHB Inflict Wounds**

```json
{
  "id": { "owner": "spell:phb:inflict-wounds", "slot": "attack-roll:primary" },
  "payload": {
    "kind": "ATTACK_ROLL",
    "attackKind": "MELEE_SPELL",
    "actor": "CASTER",
    "attacksPerActivation": { "minimum": 1, "maximum": 1, "independent": true },
    "targeting": { "eligibleTargets": ["CREATURE"], "selectionPerAttack": "ONE" },
    "timing": "DURING_CASTING"
  },
  "evidenceAssociations": [
    { "evidenceId": "<source-entry-evidence>", "claimPath": "/payload", "role": "EFFECT_ROLE" }
  ],
  "review": { "humanDecision": null },
  "licensing": { "status": "NEEDS_REVIEW" }
}
```

Its separate `DamageMechanic` would bind to this attack revision with
`AppliesOnAttackRollOutcome(outcome = HIT)`. Evidence for the attack and damage still points to the
exact source phrases in `/spell/192/entries/0`.

**B. Three independent ranged attacks, same or different targets — PHB Scorching Ray**

### Recommended Step 7.4.4G scope

Start with a one-owner pilot for `spell:phb:inflict-wounds` only, after a complete owner-level
candidate list is regenerated read-only from pinned source and joined to the current inventory.
Implement the smallest form of `AttackRollMechanic`, `AttackKind`, actor role, fixed independent
attack count, creature target selection, cast-time timing, `AttackRollOutcome`, and the
revision-bound effect binding. Reuse `MechanicRecord`, `SpellTargetSubject` only for its creature
semantics, `DamageMechanic`, `SpellMechanicsEvidenceSource`, and `SpellMechanicsAssembly`. The source
attack and hit-damage phrases need separate exact evidence associations; the damage dependency must
reference the attack claim revision. Keep review pending and licensing at the current unassessed
default.

Expected immediate benefit is one new typed attack claim and a typed hit dependency for an existing
or newly extracted damage claim, without claiming corpus-wide attack coverage. A generic rule may
then be assessed against other PHB single-target, one-roll, hit-damage-only sources, but those owners
must be checked individually before adding them.

Explicitly exclude in this pilot: multiple attacks/targets; attack-count scaling; later-turn or
duration repetition; objects; weapon attacks; summoned/created actors; miss effects; critical rules;
advantage/disadvantage and roll replacement; conditions or additional hit riders; attacks only
mentioned as a restriction, trigger, example or stat-block detail. Abstain with a source-bound
unsupported diagnostic when attack kind, actor, target, count, timing, or outcome-to-effect relation
is not explicit.

Required compatibility checks for a future implementation include stable owner/slot IDs, source
revision and fingerprint integrity, exact evidence pointers/spans and claim associations, unchanged
existing claims/revisions/review/licensing, deterministic inventory and benchmark outputs in
`target/`, unchanged default and private inspection responses except the authorized new claim, and
no publication authorization. A successful benchmark match is not human verification.

## Prioritization

1. **Single attack with explicit hit damage (Inflict Wounds pilot):** low ambiguity; one actor, one
   creature target, one attack, one hit consequence; it directly tests the missing attack-to-damage
   dependency with existing damage/evidence infrastructure.
2. **Simple ranged damage attacks:** likely reusable, but screen target scope and riders individually;
   Fire Bolt names objects as well as creatures, while many rays add conditions or speed changes.
3. **Multiple independent attacks and target allocation:** high reuse potential, but needs count,
   independence, target reuse and level-scaling fields (Scorching Ray, Eldritch Blast).
4. **Hit/miss consequence trees and special critical/roll rules:** lower-frequency but semantically
   high-risk (Melf's Acid Arrow, Blade of Disaster). Require explicit outcome branches and threshold
   typing before extraction.
5. **Later-turn, created-entity, and attack-modifier families:** defer until timing/actor and
   general modifier semantics can be represented without treating a spell as the roll actor
   (Conjure Fey, Witch Bolt, Bless).

Prevalence uses the overlapping 75-owner discovery cue count only as a search signal. It does not
override the owner-level source audit. Source clarity and model fit favor the one-spell pilot over a
high-count regex batch.

## Safeguards, limitations and verification

The proposed additions would change canonical claim revisions because payloads and bindings are
revision-hashed. A future change must preserve existing IDs, revisions, evidence associations,
review/licensing state and publication safeguards; new claims must default to pending human review
and unassessed licensing. Benchmark fixtures and fingerprints must not be silently rewritten.
Private inspection may expose an authorized new internal claim only through its established private
contract; default/public REST projections and publication decisions remain unchanged.

No production code, tests, fixtures, pinned sources, inventory reports, or benchmark reports were
changed. No tests were required for this analysis-only step. The repeated deterministic JSON output
and its SHA-256 are recorded in the completion check; `git diff --check` passes.
