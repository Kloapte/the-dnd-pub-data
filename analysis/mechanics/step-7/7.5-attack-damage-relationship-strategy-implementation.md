# Step 7.5 — Generic Attack to Damage Relationship Strategy

## Result

Implemented a reusable experimental relationship strategy for explicit attack-roll HIT/MISS
outcomes and damage. It is registered beside the existing saving-throw-to-damage strategy and
uses no spell-owner or edition allowlists. The strategy describes authored mechanics; it does not
resolve attacks or execute combat. Canonical extraction, claim hashes, review decisions, licensing,
publication authorization, and default Spell responses remain unchanged. Step 7.5 remains in progress.

## Implementation

`GenericMechanicStrategy` now extracts melee and ranged spell attacks from authored attack clauses.
It emits the attack kind only when the text identifies it, uses `UNSPECIFIED` actor/timing values
when the source does not say, and retains explicit caster and cast-time wording. Explicit plural
attack counts produce an independent attack group; repeated projectile attacks are counted only
when a count is available in the same source fragment. Otherwise the strategy abstains with
`AMBIGUOUS_ATTACK_GROUPING`. It does not infer an attack from a save, ability check, or damage alone.

`AttackDamageOutcomeStrategy` is registered by `MechanicRelationshipStrategyRegistry` and operates
after mechanic extraction. It links only explicit HIT/MISS wording to damage mechanics. It supports
full, half, and no damage where authored, and can attach several outcome rules to a single damage
mechanic. It distinguishes damage by source-text offsets produced by the damage extractor. It emits
diagnostics for co-occurrence without a link, ambiguous attack pairing, unsupported outcome wording,
unresolved damage extraction, separate later activations, delayed timing, and conditional attack
chains. It never assumes that a missing MISS rule means zero damage.

The v2 relationship DTO remains additive: save relationships keep their existing `outcomes` array;
attack relationships use `attackOutcomes`. Empty attack fields are omitted from existing save
relationships. Relationship validation checks endpoint mechanic families, source-qualified owner,
source revision, evidence reciprocity, duplicate edges, and graph acyclicity. Local relationships
are deterministic and top-level; they do not create attack instances or form recursive claim graphs.

The private API continues to expose these relationships under
`?include=interpretedMechanics` → `experimentalInterpretationV2.relationships`. Mechanics, evidence,
diagnostics, and interpretation metadata remain separate. The include remains optional. A direct
default-response check for Fireball confirmed that `interpretedMechanics` is absent without the
include. The [Spell inspection API contract](../../docs/api/SPELL-INSPECTION.md) now documents the
additive relationship outcomes.

## Representative source-backed results

With `GET /api/spells/spell:phb:chill-touch?include=interpretedMechanics`, the experimental
projection keeps attack and damage as separate mechanics and exposes their authored outcome link
in `experimentalInterpretationV2.relationships` (excerpt; evidence remains in the sibling
`evidence` collection):

```json
{
  "type": "ATTACK_DAMAGE_OUTCOME",
  "sourceMechanicId": "exp-81fe57b231d8b52b2892",
  "targetMechanicId": "exp-8e7de5335b9601bb31b2",
  "evidenceIds": ["ev-78fa92e635054de6cc47"],
  "attackOutcomes": [{"outcome": "HIT", "application": "FULL_DAMAGE"}]
}
```

For `spell:phb:melf-s-acid-arrow`, separate damage mechanics retain their separate authored
outcomes: the initial damage is full on HIT and half on MISS; the delayed component is full on HIT
and has no damage on MISS. Its later timing is diagnostic-only in this bounded implementation.

- `spell:phb:chill-touch`: one ranged spell attack and a HIT → necrotic damage relationship. Its
  authored next-turn hit-point restriction is not treated as delayed damage.
- `spell:phb:scorching-ray`: no one-attack claim is fabricated from “for each ray”; its ray count is
  in a sibling source entry, outside current extraction-fragment context. The grouping remains
  diagnostic.
- `spell:phb:melf-s-acid-arrow`: the initial damage has a HIT/full and MISS/half relationship; the
  delayed component is linked only to the source-authored HIT and MISS/no-damage clauses. Its timing
  remains diagnosed as unmodeled.
- `spell:xge:ice-knife`: the piercing damage links to initial HIT. Cold explosion damage remains
  governed by the separate save relationship, not the attack relationship.
- `spell:xge:steel-wind-strike`: per-target attacks are not collapsed to one attack when their
  selected-target count/context is in sibling entries.
- `spell:xphb:chromatic-orb`: its conditional chain remains unsupported. The pipeline does not
  create a bounce relationship based on unmodeled damage-dice matching.
- `spell:ai:jim-s-magic-missile`: the same-fragment source explicitly creates three darts and calls
  for one attack per missile; it is represented as a three-attack independent group with a HIT-bound
  damage relationship. No critical-hit or natural-1 rule is inferred from those damage clauses.

Other PHB and XPHB attack spells also match the generic rule. PHB and XPHB remain separate owners
and retain their individual resource fingerprints.

## Diagnostics and limits

The final artifact records 18 `AMBIGUOUS_ATTACK_DAMAGE_PAIRING`, 4
`AMBIGUOUS_ATTACK_GROUPING`, 14 `ATTACK_DAMAGE_TARGET_NOT_EXTRACTED`, 4
`ATTACK_DAMAGE_OUTCOME_UNRESOLVED`, and 160 `ATTACK_WORDING_UNSUPPORTED` diagnostics. There are two
`ATTACK_DAMAGE_DELAYED_TIMING_UNMODELED`, one
`ATTACK_DAMAGE_SEPARATE_ACTIVATION_UNSUPPORTED`, and one
`CONDITIONAL_ATTACK_CHAIN_UNSUPPORTED` finding. These counts describe diagnostic occurrences, not
unique spells.

The main remaining limitation is source context split across sibling entry fragments: attack counts,
target allocations, and relationships may be clear when read as a whole spell but are not joined by
this bounded strategy. Text evidence currently points to the normalized authored fragment span,
which contains the triggering clause; it is not narrowed to a clause-only span. Relationship source
revision and endpoint evidence are validated, but a more precise evidence-span model remains useful.
Natural-language patterns outside the bounded HIT/MISS forms remain diagnostic.
