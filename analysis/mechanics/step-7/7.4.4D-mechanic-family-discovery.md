## Method

I joined each inventory record to a source spell by exact source and name, then scanned `entries`
and `entriesHigherLevel` strings and the relevant structured spell fields. Each owner is counted at
most once within each family. Counts overlap between families and must not be summed as unique
Spells. Classification counts are the existing inventory classifications, unchanged.

The counts below are cue-matched evidence owners, not automatic extraction eligibility. Structured
fields such as `spellAttack`, `conditionImmune`, and damage immunity/resistance fields are strong
source evidence. Prose cues identify explicit language but can still capture a restriction, a
referenced creature's stat block, or a conditional clause rather than a direct effect. The report
therefore distinguishes cue prevalence from semantic confidence. Representative excerpts and exact
source JSON pointers are included in the machine-readable summary. Pointers are rooted at the source
array, such as `/spell/97/entries/0`.

## Prioritization

1. **Condition immunity from structured `conditionImmune` fields â€” recommend as the next small batch.** The 12-owner cue set is structured-only; 8 owners are currently `UNSUPPORTED` and 4 `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`. The existing `GRANT_IMMUNITY` operation makes the core representation available. The batch can remain bounded by confirming source owner/revision, condition tags, affected subject, and effect duration, while abstaining when those bindings are unclear. This is a modest coverage gain with low semantic risk and no new model type required.
2. **Attack-roll resolution â€” high reusable coverage, medium/high complexity.** 75 owners have structured and/or direct prose cues (56 unsupported). Repeated attacks, hit-dependent effects, target count, and attack modifiers need a narrow payload design before extraction; the current trigger and damage types alone are insufficient.
3. **Damage protection â€” high prevalence, medium complexity.** 52 cue owners include 38 unsupported and 11 partial. Existing models cover resistance and some prevention, but reduction, save-based mitigation, temporary hit points, and resistance must remain separate operations.
4. **Visibility/senses and movement â€” medium prevalence, high model gap.** Their prose is often direct, but no current payload captures sensory capabilities or the full movement/forced displacement semantics.
5. **Summoning, terrain, transformation, and conditional endings â€” defer for focused modeling.** Counts range from 14 to 34; these families contain materially different lifecycles, area states, form changes, and effect-ending conditions. Broad extraction would risk flattening meaningful source distinctions.

Counts should guide sampling, not dictate activation. Prior to any implementation, inspect owner-level source patterns and exceptions for the proposed bounded batch, then capture compatibility baselines and preserve abstentions.

## Limitations and safeguards

- Family matching is deterministic and owner-distinct, but prose cues are discovery filters rather than adjudicated mechanics. The most likely false positives are nested creature statistics, references to restrictions on an effect, and text that names a game concept without applying it to the spell's target.
- A Spell may belong to multiple families; totals are not additive. A family cue does not alter its inventory classification or prove that an owner is production-eligible.
- Structured fields supply concise classification evidence but may omit subject, duration, alternatives, outcome, or relationship to prose. A structured tag must not be expanded into semantics absent from the authored source.
- Existing model candidates indicate representation fit only. They do not authorize new IDs, extraction, review approval, licensing changes, or publication.
- This analysis does not redesign coverage accounting. The known `/entries` assessment gap and 0 `SUPPORTED` classification remain unchanged.
