# Core Rules Action and Rule Mechanics Research

**Purpose:** Focused evidence for the ongoing Step 2, Phase 2 design review. This report makes no schema or conversion changes. Phase 3 has not begun.

## 1. Research scope and methodology

Inspected the current Action and Rule schemas, their representative examples, the complete Action and Rule source arrays, the Step 2 Phase 1 discovery report, and the existing Phase 2 design and validation report.

| Source | Complete records inspected | Distinct names | Source variation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `src/main/resources/data/actions.json` → `action` | 48 | 34 | 4 source codes: PHB 20, XPHB 18, DMG 8, XGE 2. Fourteen names occur in both PHB and XPHB. |
| `src/main/resources/data/variantrules.json` → `variantrule` | 231 | 224 | 15 source codes; 115 records are XPHB. Seven repeated names occur in different sources. |

The inspection recursively traversed every `entries` value, including strings nested in lists, tables, and section objects, and retained each record's name, source, and page. Counts below are **source-record memberships**, not counts of normalized operations. A record is counted once in each applicable family; family counts overlap. For Rule, the five broad inventory counts use explicit, title-led subject sets, checked against the corresponding source content. The D20 Test set is further divided into mutually exclusive title groups. These counts are reproducible record inventories, not a claim that every mention in every entry has been semantically converted. The total number of atomic mechanical statements cannot be counted reliably without entry-by-entry interpretation.

The original census reports `ruleType` values C 115, O 43, V 17, VO 16, and absent 40. These are source facts only. Repository evidence still does not establish their full-word meanings, and none are inferred here.

The resulting **16 outcome occurrences across 9 records** are not 16 records. Tumble is a contested check and is not counted as a roll modifier.

### Operation overlap and reusable structure

- Advantage/disadvantage is the clearest reusable mechanic. It recurs in 9 records; roll type, actor/target, trigger, expiry, and usage limit determine its application. A small typed roll-modifier value can be reused without introducing a universal operation engine.
- Checks are similarly reusable at the input/resolution level, but contested checks, DC checks, attack rolls, saving throws, and skill-assistance procedures have different target and outcome contracts.
- Movement verbs do not establish one movement operation. Dash increases the actor's available movement; Shove moves another creature or makes it Prone; Shove Aside selects a different space; Disengage changes opportunity-attack eligibility; Ready schedules a later response.
- Ending Concentration and escaping a grapple both end an ongoing state, but one targets a Status and one ends a Condition. Target-type-specific operations preserve that distinction.
- Object interaction has a real common core, but activation requirements and action timing separate Use an Object/Utilize from magic-item activation and shield handling.

### Timing evidence affecting operations

The Action inventory has time values on 47 of 48 records. It contains 50 supplied timing values: 37 action, 3 reaction, 4 bonus-action, 4 `Varies`, and 2 `Free`. Mark has no source timing. Identify a Spell (XGE p. 85) supplies both action and reaction timing; Overrun and Tumble (DMG pp. 272) each supply both action and bonus-action timing. These three records have explicit alternatives rather than a single duration. The schema can encode alternatives through `varies.options`, but that collapses “several explicit alternatives” into the same representation as a source `Varies` value.

The four source `Varies` records are Cast a Spell (PHB p. 192), Improvising an Action (PHB p. 193; XPHB p. 15), and Other Activity (PHB p. 190). Cast a Spell identifies action, reaction, minutes, and hours; Improvising an Action leaves the DM to determine the time; Other Activity includes interactions during movement or an action as well as some action-cost interactions. A required finite `varies.options` array cannot fully represent the latter two without inventing a closed list. Preserve absent timing for Mark. **Investigate** whether explicit alternatives and open-ended `Varies` need separate forms; do not guess options.

## 3. Action reuse and schema coverage assessment

The Action schema distinguishes capabilities from operations, which is a useful boundary, but capability labels alone do not encode the resulting mechanics. Of the eight operation families above, the current operation vocabulary directly covers only two: `GRANT_ATTACK_ADVANTAGE` (Mark) and `END_STATUS` (End Concentration, two edition records). Thus **3 of 48 source records** contain a directly expressible operation, although those records may also contain other mechanics. The remaining named operation patterns have no operation representation or only a capability/requirement label.

The existing operations are not broadly over-fragmented; the exception is `GRANT_ATTACK_ADVANTAGE`, whose fields hard-code Mark's trigger and marked target despite shared modifier behavior elsewhere. `END_STATUS` is appropriately explicit for Status targets but should not silently stand in for Condition termination. Current coverage cannot represent the supported results of Grapple, Shove, Hide, Stabilize, forced movement, object activation, or many check outcomes without dropping mechanics.

**Assessment:** Keep capabilities as action identity/selection facts. Consolidate the Mark-specific advantage operation around a typed roll modifier, and extend only with domain-specific movement, state, check, item, and recovery operations evidenced above. Do not merge mechanics solely because they mention the same roll or movement.

## 5. Rule overlap and schema coverage assessment

Rule concepts overlap by mechanics even when their titles fall in different families. The title-led groups have four exact source-record overlaps: Critical Hit and Death Saving Throw (D20 Test × Damage/State), Spell Attack (D20 Test × Spell/Magic), and Teleportation (Spatial × Spell/Magic). A Rule record may also contain several independent mechanics under one identity. In the full 231-record census, 78 contain nested named `entries` sections, 61 contain tables, and 25 contain lists. Those structural counts do not themselves prove a mechanic count, but several records clearly aggregate many procedures:

| Source record | Observed structure | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action Options (DMG p. 271) | Six named action procedures: Climb onto a Bigger Creature, Disarm, Mark, Overrun, Shove Aside, and Tumble. | This is a source grouping of distinct procedures; a single Rule subtype cannot describe all six. Evaluate child Rule identities or explicit composition during design. |
| Initiative Variants (DMG p. 270) | Six named sections including initiative score, side initiative, speed factor, modifiers, rolling, and turns. | One initiative family can contain different procedures and optional variants; do not conflate them into a single roll modifier. |
| Travel at Sea (GoS p. 199) | Seventeen named sections and 17 tables, including pace, activities, navigation, threats, repairs, hazards, conflict, fire, fog, infestation, and storms. | This is a multi-step subsystem with multiple domains and dependencies, not one executable Rule subtype. |
| Ships in Combat (GoS p. 198) | Seven named sections covering initiative, officer actions, crew, casualties, and crashing. | The top-level source record contains distinct actions, participants, and outcomes. |
| Tool Proficiencies (XGE p. 78) | 154 nested named sections and 26 tables. | This record mixes general check/tool principles with individual tool procedures, sample DCs, and use cases. It needs submodel analysis before one canonical Rule entity can represent it. |
| Downtime Revisited (XGE p. 123) | Nine named sections including rivals, goals, assets, plans, activity resolution, complications, and examples. | “Downtime” is a family of campaign procedures; one broad subtype would be too coarse. |

The five current alternatives provide a useful initial vocabulary for several common mechanics, but they are structurally insufficient for the complete Rule source set. The `oneOf` requires exactly one `rule` object and has no explicit collection of typed substructures, so it cannot represent multiple concepts in a single record. Its current bodies also cannot represent modifiers, restrictions, exceptions, resources, damage/healing, social procedures, or multi-step optional subsystems. Adding a generic `effects` or prose field would violate the canonical-data boundary. A typed composition of reviewed, cohesive submodels remains a candidate; broadening the union without named responsibilities would not solve the overlap.

Some Rule records are primarily definitional or organizational rather than independently executable, such as Adventure (XPHB p. 360), Campaign (XPHB p. 362), Character Sheet (XPHB p. 363), Creature (XPHB p. 364), Stat Block (XPHB p. 374), and action/downtime section groupings. Other records with similarly broad names contain concrete procedures. The source title alone is insufficient to classify a whole record as descriptive; count a mechanic only after interpreting its actual entry content.

## 6. Evidence-backed recommendations

1. **Action:** retain the Action domain and capability layer. Reframe Mark's `GRANT_ATTACK_ADVANTAGE` as a reusable typed roll-modifier operation, preserving its opportunity-attack trigger, marked target, expiry, and per-turn limit. Keep attack variants, forced movement, condition/status changes, object/equipment changes, spell/item activation, and recovery as distinct typed responsibilities where their outcomes differ.
2. **Action timing:** preserve optional timing. Distinguish source `Varies`, explicit multiple timing values, `Free`, and missing timing if conversion evidence requires it. The source supports action, bonus-action, reaction, and minute/hour timing; it does not support filling a default for Mark or a closed list for open-ended improvisation.
3. **Rule:** recognize `D20_TEST` as a plausible family because XPHB defines it and the source separates ability checks, attack rolls, and saves. Keep these subtypes distinct. Review whether a shared roll context can be reused without forcing source editions into an unsupported common execution rule.
4. **Rule composition:** do not require each Rule record to map to one family. Action Options, Travel at Sea, Ships in Combat, Tool Proficiencies, and Downtime Revisited contain multiple procedures or conceptual sections. Consider explicit typed composition or child Rule identities after determining which headings are independent mechanics versus editorial organization.
5. **Rule breadth:** current Movement and Cover structures align with a subset of identified records, but have not been shown to cover their full behavior. The source has material damage/health, spell/magic, resource/recovery, social, creature, and campaign families absent from the current alternatives. Add only evidence-backed families and keep optional subsystems domain-specific.
6. **Source classification:** leave C/O/V/VO unresolved. Their counts and co-occurrences are not a semantic key and do not justify family membership or runtime toggles.

## 7. Open questions requiring design decisions

1. Should roll modifiers be a small reusable value shared by Action and Rule subtypes, with action-specific triggers and expirations kept on the owning Action?
2. Should applying or ending a Condition use distinct `APPLY_CONDITION` / `END_CONDITION` operations while `END_STATUS` remains specific to Status targets?
3. Should action timing distinguish open-ended `Varies` from explicit source alternatives such as Identify a Spell, Overrun, and Tumble? What representation preserves the Other Activity and Improvising Action cases without guessed options?
4. For Rule, should `D20_TEST` be a parent family with explicit Ability Check, Attack Roll, and Saving Throw subtypes? If so, should Death Saving Throw remain a specialized subtype, and how are older-source initiative/check conventions kept edition-specific?
5. How should multi-procedure Rule records be composed: multiple typed mechanics within one Rule identity, or stable child Rule identities for explicitly named procedures? The source organization alone does not settle this.
6. Which Rule families should receive dedicated contracts first among damage/health, area/target geometry, recovery/resources, spell effects, social procedures, and downtime/campaign systems? The current evidence shows these families occur but does not approve a normalization order or runtime execution scope.
7. What repository evidence, if any, authoritatively defines the meanings of `ruleType` C, O, V, and VO? Until then, retain them as unresolved source facts outside canonical classifications.
